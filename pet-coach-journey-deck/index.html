Pet Coach

An AI-assisted triage and follow-up companion for pet owners and clinic staff.

A navigable product walkthrough — horizontal for the journey, vertical for depth.

Pet Coach is a fictional product used for class purposes. It is framed as a support tool and does not replace veterinary care. Urgent or worsening symptoms should be escalated to a clinic or emergency veterinary service.

Before the Visit · Step 1

Pet owner starts AI triage in Pet Coach.

01
The owner opens the app because something seems off — the dog has been low energy, eating less, or the cat is coughing more than usual. Pet Coach starts a guided conversation to capture what's happening, while keeping clear safety boundaries about what the AI is and isn't.
↓Optional deep dive · UX, data, safety, prototype
This is where most users meet Pet Coach for the first time. Emphasize that the entry point is conversational on purpose — pet owners often don't know what's relevant to mention. Mention that there are four vertical slides: example chat, what gets captured, safety, and a UX mockup.
UX deep dive

Example pet owner chat.

9:41●●●●
Pet Coach Triage
Maple has been low energy and eating less today.
I'm sorry Maple isn't feeling well. To help, can I ask a few questions?
When did you first notice the lower energy?
Since yesterday afternoon.
Any vomiting, diarrhea, or trouble breathing?
No vomiting, but she didn't finish breakfast.
Got it. A few more questions, then I'll suggest the next step.

What's happening here

  • Pet Coach asks one question at a time instead of a long form.
  • The owner mentions plain-language symptoms; Pet Coach maps those to structured fields in the background.
  • The conversation is paced to avoid alarming the owner while still capturing what the clinic will need.
  • If the owner mentions an urgent symptom, the conversation jumps directly to escalation guidance.

Safety deep dive

Safety boundaries and escalation.

Pet Coach is not a veterinarian. When the owner describes urgent signs — difficulty breathing, severe bleeding, suspected poisoning, collapse, repeated vomiting, seizures — the AI stops triaging and surfaces emergency guidance.
  • Urgent symptom keywords trigger an immediate escalation prompt, not more questions.
  • The escalation prompt shows a clinic phone number and an emergency veterinary care link.
  • Pet Coach does not diagnose — it captures what the owner reports and suggests a next action.
  • For ambiguous cases, the recommendation defaults toward "schedule a visit" rather than "monitor at home."
  • All escalations are logged for clinic review.

UI cues to notice

  • The clarifying questions are short and binary where possible.
  • Safety reminders appear inline as a distinct yellow bubble — visually different from advice.
  • The chat input is a single line; full-screen forms are avoided.
  • The header always shows the pet's name, so the owner is reminded the AI is working with their pet's context.
Before the Visit · Step 2

Pet Coach captures symptoms and recommends a next action.

02
Once Pet Coach has enough context, it suggests a next step ranging from monitor at home to contact emergency veterinary care. The recommendation is offered with appropriate caveats and never overstated as a diagnosis.
↓Optional deep dive · actions, uncertainty, guardrails
This is the moment where AI value becomes visible to the user. Emphasize the range of recommendations — Pet Coach should be willing to say "this looks like a routine concern" and equally willing to say "please go to the ER now."
Clinical deep dive

Possible next actions.

Monitor at home
Routine, mild symptoms. Care guidance and a check-back window are provided.
Schedule a routine appointment
Concerning but non-urgent — book within the next several days.
Schedule a same-day visit
Worsening or persistent symptoms — book the soonest available slot.
Contact emergency veterinary care
Urgent presentation — Pet Coach surfaces emergency contact info immediately.

The recommendation is paired with a one-sentence rationale the owner can read aloud to clinic staff.

Safety deep dive

Confidence and uncertainty handling.

  • If the model's confidence is low, Pet Coach defaults to recommending a visit rather than home monitoring.
  • The owner sees plain-language uncertainty cues — "this could be a few different things; a clinic can help narrow it down."
  • Numeric confidence scores are not shown to owners. They tend to mislead.
  • The clinic does see internal uncertainty signals on the staff review screen.
  • If symptoms escalate during the conversation, the recommendation is upgraded immediately.
Design rule: when in doubt, route to a human. Over-recommending a visit is far less harmful than under-recommending one.
Safety deep dive

Clinical guardrails.

  • Pet Coach does not name a diagnosis. It describes what was reported and suggests a next step.
  • Medication advice is limited to clinic-issued prescriptions and dosing instructions already in the record.
  • Generic web information is not surfaced as advice unless tagged as educational and clinic-approved.
  • Owner-reported information is flagged as such in the pre-visit summary so staff don't mistake it for clinical observation.
  • Any output that crosses into diagnosis or new clinical recommendation is blocked by a downstream guardrail check.
Before the Visit · Step 3

Pet Coach saves a pre-visit summary for the clinic.

03
The triage transcript is condensed into a structured pre-visit summary that travels with the appointment. Clinic staff see a clean, reviewable handoff — not a wall of chat.
↓Optional deep dive · data model, privacy, architecture
The handoff is where the product earns its keep — staff time saved, less repetition for the owner. Mention that the architecture vertical shows how the chat-to-case flow works at a system level.
Data deep dive

Transcript vs structured summary.

Raw transcript
The literal back-and-forth of the chat. Useful for audit and for staff who want to see what the owner said in their own words.
AI-generated structured summary
A condensed view: pet, symptoms, duration, urgency, owner concerns, suggested visit type. The default view for clinic staff.
Confirmed staff notes
What clinic staff actually entered after reviewing the AI summary. This is the source of truth for the appointment.
Owner-reported flags
Clearly tagged as owner-reported, not clinical. These help staff decide which questions to ask first.

Safety deep dive

Privacy, audit, and review considerations.

  • Transcripts and summaries are scoped to the pet and the chosen clinic — not shared broadly.
  • Owner consent for sharing the chat with the clinic is explicit and revocable.
  • All AI-generated summaries are versioned and timestamped.
  • Staff edits create a new note layered on top of the AI summary, never silent overwrites.
  • Logs distinguish between "AI said," "owner reported," and "staff confirmed."
  • Periodic audit reviews compare AI summaries to staff-confirmed notes to catch drift.

Before the Visit · Step 4

Pet owner schedules a clinic visit.

04
The triage outcome carries straight into scheduling. Pet Coach pre-selects a likely visit type, surfaces the right urgency band of available slots, and confirms with calendar reminders.
↓Optional deep dive · types, urgency, prototype
This step is the easy win — going from triage to a confirmed appointment without re-entering symptoms. Ask the audience: how many of you have ever had to repeat your concern three times to schedule a doctor visit?
Clinical deep dive

Appointment type selection.

Wellness concern
Routine check-ins, behavior, diet, preventive care.
Sick visit
Symptoms reported, non-urgent. Standard appointment slot.
Same-day urgent visit
Concerning but not emergency. Soonest available slot.
Follow-up visit
Reviewing prior diagnostics, prescriptions, or care plan progress.

The triage rationale pre-selects the visit type, but the owner can change it.

Data deep dive

Availability and urgency matching.

  • Routine concerns see a 7-day rolling availability window.
  • Sick visits see a 2-3 day window, with same-day slots highlighted.
  • Same-day urgent visits surface only today's and tomorrow morning's slots.
  • If no urgent slots are open, Pet Coach surfaces the clinic's "call us directly" path.
  • Slot availability is queried live from the clinic's scheduling system.
UX deep dive

Confirmation and reminders.

  • Confirmation is sent via the channel the owner prefers — push, SMS, or email.
  • The reminder includes the owner-friendly version of the AI summary.
  • "Bring this with you" prompts list things like medication bottles and the date symptoms started.
  • If symptoms change before the visit, Pet Coach prompts a quick check-in to update the summary.
  • A 24-hour and a 1-hour reminder are sent by default.

During the Visit · Step 6

Clinic staff reviews the AI pre-visit summary.

06
Staff open the pet's case in the clinic system. They see the AI summary first, with a clear distinction between AI-generated text and what they are about to confirm. The point is to save time, not skip diligence.
↓Optional deep dive · summary, confirm, risks, prototype
The honest framing: the AI saves staff time but doesn't replace their judgment. The vertical labeled "Risks of relying on an AI summary" is the one to descend into if the audience skews skeptical.
Data deep dive

Staff-facing pre-visit summary.

  • Pet identity, owner contact, and chosen clinic are at the top.
  • Reported symptoms appear as structured fields, not paragraph form.
  • The AI summary sits in its own panel, clearly labeled "AI summary — review before confirming."
  • Owner-reported urgency and triage outcome are visible at a glance.
  • A link to the full transcript is available, but not the default view.
Clinical deep dive

What staff should confirm.

  • The pet identity and reason for visit match what the owner says in the room.
  • Any flagged "open questions" the AI surfaced have been answered with the owner.
  • Severity of reported symptoms still matches what the owner is describing now.
  • No new urgent signs have appeared between scheduling and arrival.
  • Allergies, medications, and weight are current.
Convention: staff confirm by adding their note. They do not overwrite the AI summary — that history matters for audit.
Safety deep dive

Risks of relying on an AI summary.

  • Misinterpretation: the AI summarizes what the owner said — not always what the owner meant.
  • Missing context: body language, demeanor, and the pet's appearance aren't captured.
  • Recency: symptoms can change between triage and arrival; staff must re-confirm.
  • Anchoring bias: reading the AI summary first may bias staff toward its framing.
  • Mitigation: open questions, audit logs, mandatory staff-confirmed notes, and periodic AI-vs-confirmed comparisons.

During the Visit · Step 7

Veterinarian examines the pet and orders diagnostics if needed.

07
The vet does what only the vet can do: physical exam, judgment, and decision about whether to run diagnostics. Pet Coach plays no role in this step except as a passive context source if the vet wants it.
↓Optional deep dive · in-house, lab, branching, workflow
Use this step to acknowledge that AI doesn't help with the most clinical part of the visit. The walkthrough is honest about where the AI is silent.
Clinical deep dive

In-house diagnostics path.

  • Run during the visit on equipment in the clinic.
  • Examples: rapid blood chemistry, in-house imaging review, basic urinalysis.
  • Results are typically available before the owner leaves.
  • Vet discusses results with the owner in person and adjusts the care plan in real time.
  • Results are written into the case record immediately.
Clinical deep dive

Reference lab path.

  • Samples are sent to an outside reference lab.
  • Results return hours to days later.
  • Vet typically reviews the results, then communicates them to the owner asynchronously.
  • The owner may have follow-up questions — this is exactly where Pet Coach reappears.
  • Results are appended to the case record when they arrive.
Clinical deep dive

Decision points and branching logic.

In-house diagnostics

When the vet needs answers now.
  • Rapid panels.
  • Bedside imaging review.
  • Same-visit treatment plan.

Reference lab

When more depth or specialty testing is needed.
  • Cytology, culture, advanced panels.
  • Async results pathway.
  • Owner contacted later by clinic.

Both paths feed back into the same case record, so Pet Coach can later reference results from either source.

Workflow deep dive

From diagnostics order to result.

  1. Vet orders diagnostic Order is created in the clinic system; sample collected.
  2. Sample routed In-house instrument or reference lab pickup.
  3. Result generated In-house: minutes. Reference lab: hours to days.
  4. Vet reviews and signs off Adds clinical interpretation; decides on care plan changes.
  5. Result attached to case Available to clinic staff and, with consent, to Pet Coach for owner-facing follow-up.
  6. Owner notified Via Pet Coach push, SMS, or call — clinic preference applies.
During & After · Step 8

Diagnostic results are produced or returned.

08
Results land in the case record. From here, two things happen: the vet decides what changes (if any) the care plan needs, and Pet Coach gains new context it can use — carefully — to answer follow-up questions later.
↓Optional deep dive · in-house, lab, data flow, safety
Two themes here: latency (in-house vs. lab) and the safety boundary between explanation and diagnosis. Pet Coach explains things the vet has already decided — it does not generate new clinical conclusions.
Clinical deep dive

In-house results during visit.

  • Available before the owner leaves the clinic.
  • Discussed in person — typically the highest-trust touchpoint of the visit.
  • Care plan adjustments happen in the room.
  • Pet Coach is intentionally quiet during this step.
Clinical deep dive

Reference lab results after visit.

  • Arrive after the visit, sometimes days later.
  • The vet writes a brief, owner-friendly summary alongside the technical result.
  • Pet Coach uses that owner-friendly summary as its basis for any future explanation — not the raw result alone.
  • If the result is significantly outside expectations, the clinic contacts the owner directly first.

Safety deep dive

Explanation, not diagnosis.

Hard rule: Pet Coach can explain what the clinic has already decided. It cannot generate new clinical conclusions from raw results.
  • If results aren't yet reviewed by a vet, Pet Coach declines to discuss them.
  • If the owner asks something outside what the vet has documented, Pet Coach says so and offers to message the clinic.
  • Worsening or unexpected symptoms always escalate to a clinic contact path.
  • The escalation defaults to the clinic's preferred contact method, not a generic chatbot loop.
During the Visit · Step 9

Veterinarian prescribes medication if needed.

09
The vet writes the prescription and an owner-friendly summary of dosing, duration, and side effects to watch for. The prescription record is the source of truth Pet Coach will refer to later — Pet Coach does not invent or alter dosing instructions.
A short, important slide. Use it to underline that the prescription record is canonical — Pet Coach can answer "what should I do if I missed a dose" only because the dosing instructions exist in writing already.
After the Visit · Step 10

Pet owner returns home with care instructions.

10
The owner leaves with a written care plan: medications, dosing schedule, things to watch for, when to call the clinic, and when to schedule a follow-up. Pet Coach now has all the context it needs to answer educational questions without overstepping.
The "after the visit" phase begins. Set up the audience for the follow-up step, where the AI's value reappears in a different form: not triage, but patient education and continuity.
After the Visit · Step 11

Pet owner later asks Pet Coach follow-up questions.

11
Days later, questions come up that don't quite warrant a clinic call. Pet Coach answers them by drawing from care instructions, prescription details, diagnostic results, and clinic guidance — and by knowing when to escalate.
↓Optional deep dive · 4 question types, prototype, RAG context
This is the slide where the entire architecture story pays off. The follow-up is only safe because earlier steps captured the right context. If the audience asks "where's the AI value?", point them at this step.
Question type

"What do these lab results mean?"

Want me to read you the short summary they wrote, or do you have a specific question I can pass to the clinic?

Pet Coach reads back the vet-authored summary — it does not interpret raw values.

Question type

"What should I do if I missed a dose?"

9:02●●●●
Pet CoachFollow-up · Maple
I missed Maple's morning dose. What should I do?
Maple's prescription says to give the next dose as soon as possible, unless it's close to the next scheduled time — in that case, skip the missed one and continue with the regular schedule. Don't double up.
When to call the clinicIf Maple seems unwell or you're unsure, please call Westside Clinic — I can dial them for you.

The dosing rule comes verbatim from the prescription. Anything outside that scope is escalated.

Question type

"Is this side effect expected?"

  • Pet Coach checks the clinic-provided side-effect list for the prescribed medication.
  • If the reported symptom is on the "expected, monitor" list, Pet Coach confirms and gives monitoring guidance.
  • If the symptom is on the "stop and call clinic" list, Pet Coach escalates immediately.
  • If the symptom is not on either list, Pet Coach does not guess — it offers to message the clinic.
Boundary: Pet Coach does not invent reassurance. Silence in the source data means escalation, not improvisation.
Safety deep dive

When to call the clinic.

  • Any worsening of the original symptom.
  • New symptoms not on the expected list.
  • Suspected adverse reaction to medication.
  • Owner uncertainty — Pet Coach treats "I don't know what to do" as a signal to escalate.
  • Anything urgent: trouble breathing, collapse, severe bleeding, suspected poisoning.
Design rule: escalation paths are always one tap away — never buried.
